    Rainbow

    Overview

    Rainbow is a trick-taking card game of sets, runs, and timing.

    Each trick:

    • Every player plays 1 combo from their hand
    • Players then claim point cards from the middle
    • The player who played the biggest combo chooses first

    After each trick:

    • All played cards become the point cards for the next trick

    The game continues until multiple players run out of cards.

    The player with the most points wins.

    Goal

    Make a rainbow...

    ...and collect the pot of gold at the end!

    Components

    • 60 Rainbow cards
      • Ranks 1–6
      • 10 of each rank

    Playable Combos

    Single

    • Any 1 card

    ---

    Set

    • 2 or more cards of the same rank

    ---

    Run

    • 2 or more cards of consecutive ranks

    Examples:

    • 2, 3
    • 1, 2, 3, 4
    • 3, 4, 5, 6

    Setup

    1. Shuffle the deck.
    2. Deal 1 face-up card per player into the middle.
    3. Arrange them from lowest to highest.

    Deal cards based on player count:

    Players Face-Up Cards Hand Size
    3 3 14
    4 4 14
    5 5 11
    6 6 9

    3-player game:

    • Set leftover cards aside face-down

    2-player game:

    • See 2-Player Variant
    1. The player who most recently saw a rainbow leads the first trick.

    Playing a Trick

    The lead player:

    • Plays any legal combo from their hand

    Play continues clockwise.

    Each player must play 1 combo.

    Combo Restrictions

    At the start of a trick:

    • Any combo may be played

    Once a Set has been played:

    • Players may only play:
      • A Single
      • A Set

    Once a Run has been played:

    • Players may only play:
      • A Single
      • A Run

    Important:

    • You do NOT need to beat previously played combos
    • You only need to play a legal combo
    • You may always play a Single
    • If you have no other legal play:
      • You must play a Single

    Scoring a Trick

    After all players have played:

    Players claim point cards from the middle.

    Players choose in order:

    • Biggest combo first
    • Smallest combo last

    Biggest combo = most cards played

    Tie Breakers

    If players played combos of the same size:

    1st Tie Breaker

    • Highest individual card

    2nd Tie Breaker

    • Earlier played combo

    Point Cards

    First Trick Only

    Each player chooses:

    • 1 single point card

    Do NOT stack pairs during the first trick.

    All Later Tricks

    Each player chooses either:

    • 1 single

    OR

    • 1 pair of matching rank

    Each card is worth:

    • Points equal to its rank

    Examples:

    • Rank 2 = 2 points
    • Pair of 5s = 10 points

    Players keep point cards in a face-down score pile.

    Players may look at their own score piles at any time.

    Preparing the Next Trick

    After scoring:

    1. Move all played cards to the middle.
    1. Organize cards:
    • Pair matching ranks
    • Leave extras as singles

    Example:

    • Five 3s become:
      • Two pairs
      • One single
    1. Arrange all singles and pairs:
    • Lowest total points → highest total points
    1. If there are more point piles than players:
    • Discard piles from the lowest end
    • Continue until there are equal piles and players

    Next Lead Player

    The player who played the biggest combo:

    • Leads the next trick

    If that player has no cards left:

    • The next player clockwise leads instead

    Game End

    At the end of a trick:

    If only 1 player is out of cards:

    • The game continues

    That player:

    • Skips future turns
    • Can no longer claim point cards

    ---

    If 2 or more players are out of cards:

    • The game ends immediately

    Players total all points.

    Highest score wins.

    Tie:

    • Victory is shared

    2-Player Variant

    Setup

    1. Deal 4 face-up cards to the middle.
    2. Deal 2 piles of 14 cards to each player.

    Each player:

    • Takes 1 pile into hand
    • Keeps the other face-down without looking

    Gameplay

    Each trick:

    • Each player plays 2 combos
    • One at a time
    • Starting with the lead player

    If a Set or Run is played:

    • Both players must follow it
    • Or play a Single

    Each combo scores separately.

    Each combo may claim:

    • A single

    OR

    • A pair

    Empty Hand Rule

    If a player runs out of cards:

    • They immediately take their face-down pile

    If they run out again:

    • The game ends

    The other player scores:

    • +1 point per card left in hand

    Rank does not matter.

    Tips & Clarifications

    • For the first trick:
      • All point cards stay as singles
    • You never have to beat previous combos
    • Singles are always legal
    • There may be fewer point piles than players
      • Some players may score 0 points
    • Largest possible run:
      • 6 cards (1–6)
    • Largest possible set:
      • 10 cards

    Strategy Tips

    • Bigger combos score earlier—but empty your hand faster.
    • Singles are flexible and can save awkward hands.
    • Sometimes taking a smaller point card now creates a stronger middle for later.
    • Watch who is running low on cards.
